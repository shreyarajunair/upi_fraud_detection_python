Title: UPI Fraud Detection: A Comparative Study of Machine Learning and Deep Sequence Models

Abstract

The rapid adoption of the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) has revolutionized digital transactions, making seamless real-time money transfers possible. However, this unprecedented volume of digital payments has also attracted malicious actors, leading to an alarming increase in financial fraud. The significance of this project lies in developing an effective mechanism for "UPI Fraud Detection" to safeguard users and maintain the integrity of digital financial ecosystems. Given the high velocity and extreme class imbalance inherent in financial transaction data, accurately identifying fraudulent patterns among millions of legitimate transactions is both a critical necessity and a monumental challenge.

In the realm of related works, traditional machine learning algorithms have long been the standard for anomaly and fraud detection. Approaches utilizing ensemble methods, such as Random Forest and XGBoost, are widely documented for their robustness in handling tabular data and applying oversampling techniques like SMOTE. While these models handle tabular features well, they often evaluate transactions in isolation, thereby missing the nuanced, time-dependent behaviors of users. Recent literature has increasingly pointed toward deep learning architectures—specifically Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for spatial feature extraction and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for sequential modeling—as a means to better capture the complex, temporal dynamics of fraudulent activities.

The proposed system involves a comprehensive comparative analysis of traditional ensemble techniques against advanced deep learning models for detecting UPI fraud. The study evaluates Random Forest, XGBoost, a Vanilla LSTM-CNN, and a Sequential LSTM-CNN model on a recent dataset of UPI transactions, incorporating feature engineering of temporal and user-proxy data. Experimental results demonstrate the extreme difficulty of the task, with baseline tree-based models (Random Forest and XGBoost) failing to catch any fraudulent transactions at their respective thresholds. Conversely, the proposed deep learning approaches, specifically the Vanilla and Sequential LSTM-CNN architectures, successfully identified actual fraud cases (catching 8 and 6 cases, respectively). This proves that sequence-aware architectures offer superior sensitivity and are a more promising approach for real-time transaction monitoring systems.

Keywords: UPI Fraud Detection, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, LSTM-CNN, Sequential Data, XGBoost, Random Forest, Anomaly Detection.
